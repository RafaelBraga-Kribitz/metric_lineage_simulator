# Worked example: what to test first

Generated by `cd driver_tree_studio && npm run example` (seed 42, 20,000 draws per edge). Do not edit by hand.

**Question.** Which guess about Contribution Profit is most worth testing first?

**Baseline.** contribution_profit = €48,000 per period (ILLUSTRATIVE seed).

**Method.** For each modeled edge with a declared elasticity range, move its driver by the planned change, sample only that edge's elasticity, and measure the P90 − P10 spread of the north star (value of information). Divide by the declared relative test cost.

| Rank | Edge | Grade | Planned change | P10 | P50 | P90 | VoI (P90−P10) | Test cost | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `page_load_speed → product_view_rate` | estimated | -10% | €49,534 | €51,830 | €55,366 | €5,832 | 2 | 2,916 |
| 2 | `email_capture_rate → returning_sessions` | illustrative | +10% | €48,214 | €48,480 | €48,747 | €534 | 1 | 534 |
| — | `average_order_value → customer_ltv` | illustrative | — | — | — | — | not_on_path | 3 | 0 |
| — | `loyalty_membership → customer_ltv` | none | — | — | — | — | needs_guesstimate | 5 | — |

**Decision.** Test Page Load Speed (s) → Product View Rate first: its declared range blurs the planned outcome most per unit of test cost (5.5× the next guess). This ranks what to learn, not what to do.

**Sources behind graded ranges.**

- `page_load_speed → product_view_rate`: Deloitte Digital for Google — Milliseconds Make Millions (web.dev case study) (2020) https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions
- `page_load_speed → product_view_rate`: Think with Google — page load time statistics (2017) https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/data/page-load-time-statistics

> This interval is the uncertainty in your GUESS, not evidence. A distribution looks like data and is not. Its only purpose is to help you decide what to test, never to estimate impact.

Test costs are declared relative effort units (illustrative). Hypothesized edges cannot be ranked until they get a range.
